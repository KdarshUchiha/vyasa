## 1. What Vyasa actually is (and isn't)

**Vyasa is a workshop for building "mind-bending" stories** — the kind where reality,
memory, identity, or time don't behave normally. Think *Inception*, *Recursion*,
*Annihilation*, *Shutter Island*. Novels or screenplays both work.

**What makes those stories hard to write** is not the cool idea — it's *keeping it
consistent*. When your character has amnesia in chapter 3, is left-handed in chapter 1,
and your time-loop has secret rules, it's terrifyingly easy to accidentally break your
own logic. Readers notice. It shatters the spell.

> **Vyasa's whole job:** *keep the rules so your reader gets lost, and you never do.*

**What it is NOT:**
- ❌ Not a place that writes the book *for* you (though it can help — see [The Muse](#11-the-muse)).
- ❌ Not a word processor with spell-check. It's a **consistency engine + creative structure**.
- ❌ Not something that sends your work to a server. Everything lives in **your browser**.

## 2. The one big idea

Everything in Vyasa flows into a single document called the **Story Bible**.

Think of the Story Bible as your story's **brain**. Every fact you tell it — your
central twist, your characters, the rules of your magic/technology/curse, your plot
beats — gets remembered. Then, when you write, Vyasa **checks your writing against
that brain** and warns you if you contradict yourself.

That's the loop:

```
   You define the rules  ─────►  You write the story  ─────►  Vyasa checks writing vs. rules
          ▲                                                              │
          └──────────────────  You fix (or keep) contradictions  ◄──────┘
```

You never have to hold the whole labyrinth in your head. Vyasa holds it for you.

## 3. Getting started in 60 seconds

1. **Open** https://kdarshuchiha.github.io/vyasa/
2. You'll see the **Threshold** — a welcome screen asking you to *"choose the world you will bend."*
3. **Click a genre**: Sci-Fi, Horror, Thriller, or Fantasy.
   → The entire app instantly re-colours and re-styles itself to match that mood.
   (Horror goes dark and unsettling; Sci-Fi goes cold and clean, etc.)
4. You're now inside the workshop. The screen has three parts (explained next).
5. Start typing in the first box — your working title and your core idea.

That's it. Everything **auto-saves** as you type. You can close the tab and come back;
your work will be waiting.

> 💡 **Tip:** There's no "login" and no "account." The trade-off is that your work is
> saved *in this browser on this device*. To move it or back it up, use **Export**
> (see [section 12](#12-saving-exporting--starting-over)).

## 5. The Story Bible

The right-hand panel. This is the most important thing to understand. It shows, at all times:

- **Canon Integrity meter** — a 0–100% score of how solid and consistent your story is.
  It goes **up** as you add structure, and **down** when you have unresolved contradictions.
  It's a health bar for your labyrinth.
- **Central Paradox / Theme / Unreliability** — a live summary of your core idea.
- **Rules of the Anomaly** — the laws of your world (more in [section 9](#9-the-safety-net)).
- **Canon Ledger** — your characters, places, objects, and facts.
- **Beats Mapped** — how many plot beats you've created.
- **The Muse** — optional AI helper at the bottom.

Everything here updates the instant you change something. You never "save" manually —
it's automatic.

## 6. Chamber 1 — Genesis

*(Phase I: The Genesis of Disorientation)*

This is where you forge the **seed** of your story.

### ✨ Shortcut: Begin from a Logline (optional)

At the very top of Genesis is a **"Begin from a Logline"** box. If you have even a
one-sentence idea, this is the fastest way to start:

1. Type your **logline** — one sentence. *e.g. "A grieving architect discovers the city
   she's designing is a prison built from her own suppressed memories."*
2. Click **"✦ Reveal the Synopsis."** Vyasa (using your Muse key) imagines a whole
   *bible-to-be*: a springboard synopsis, a possible central paradox, theme, canon
   characters, rules, and beats — shown to you as **inspiration**. Nothing is saved yet.
3. If you like the direction, click **"🏛 Build the Bible from this →"** and Vyasa
   **fills in the fields for you** — paradox, theme, unreliability, Canon Ledger, Rules,
   and the Map's beats.
4. Don't like it? Click **"↻ Imagine another"** for a fresh take.

**This is completely optional and non-destructive:**
- It needs your **Muse key** (Story Bible → The Muse). Without a key, just fill the
  fields manually as described below.
- If your Bible already has content, Building **asks first** — it can either fill only
  the empty fields (keeping your work) or overwrite the core fields. Your call.
- **Everything it generates is a draft you then edit.** It's a starting point to open
  your imagination, not a finished story. You remain the architect.

Prefer to start from scratch? Ignore that box entirely and fill in the four fields below.

### Working Title
Just a name for now. Change it anytime.

### The Anomaly (your Central Paradox) — **the most important field**
This is the **one impossible thing** your whole story is built around. Not the plot —
the *warp in reality*.

> **Good example:** *"A detective investigating a murder slowly realises the victim,
> the killer, and the only witness are all himself, at different points in a collapsing
> timeline."*

Notice it's a **contradiction that can't be easily resolved**. That tension is the engine.

**Newbie tip:** If you're stuck, finish this sentence: *"What if [something impossible]
were true, and one person had to live inside it?"*

### Thematic Resonance (the "why")
Beyond the cool twist — what **human question** does your story make the reader feel?

> *"Is your identity anything more than the story you tell yourself, in order?"*

This keeps your mind-bender from being a hollow gimmick.

### The Unreliable Mechanism
*How* will you make the reader unsure what's true? Click **1 or 2** of the chips
(Memory-gapped narrator, Shifting environment, Fractured timeline, etc.).

> ⚠️ **Vyasa won't let you pick more than 2 on purpose.** Using every trick at once is
> the #1 rookie mistake — it becomes noise, not mystery. Restraint = craft.

When you're happy, click **"Anchor the Paradox →"**. It moves you to the Map.

## 7. Chamber 2 — The Map

*(Phase II: The Labyrinth's Blueprint)*

Here's the key insight of mind-bending stories:

> **The order events happen ≠ the order the reader learns about them.**

*Memento* is told backwards. *Inception* nests timelines. That gap is where
disorientation lives. The Map lets you **design and see** it.

### Add your beats
**Type a plot beat** in the box (e.g. *"The witness's testimony contradicts the
timestamp"*) and press **Enter**. Add as many as you like. Each beat has a **reveal
marker** — click it to cycle:
- `hidden` — the reader doesn't understand this yet
- `hinted` — you've dropped a clue
- `revealed` — the reader now fully gets it

### The Two-Track view (the heart of the Map)

The Map shows **two columns side by side**:

- **Left — Chronology:** the order events actually *happen* in your story's world.
- **Right — Reader-Order:** the order the *reader* experiences them.
- **Threads** connect the same beat across both columns.

**Drag beats in either column** to reorder that timeline:
- Drag in the **right** column to change what the reader sees first.
- Drag in the **left** column to change the true chronology.

When a beat sits in a *different* position in the two columns, its connecting thread
**turns warm-coloured and crosses** — that crossing is your disorientation, made
visible. A perfectly straight set of threads = a linear story; a tangle of crossings =
a deeply non-linear labyrinth. Hover any beat to highlight its twin and thread.

### Reveal Tension meter

Below the tracks, **Reveal Tension** scores (0–100%) how far your beats are displaced
between chronology and reader-order:
- **0%** — perfectly linear (reader learns events as they happen).
- **Mid** — "braided": the two timelines diverge meaningfully.
- **High** — deeply fractured; near-total inversion = maximum disorientation.

It's a dial for *how lost* your reader is. Tune it to taste.

### 👁 The Reader's Mind (Reader-Knowledge Simulator)

Below the tracks, Vyasa lets you **step through your story as the reader lives it** and see
the gap between what they *believe* and what's *true but hidden* — the very engine of a
mind-bending story.

Use the **slider** (or ‹ › buttons) to move to any point in reader-order. At each step:

- **🧠 The reader believes** — everything they've read and understood so far. Beats marked
  `revealed` appear solid; `hinted` beats show as dashed "clues" they've noticed but can't
  yet place.
- **🕳 The truth (hidden from them)** — what's real but the reader *doesn't* grasp yet:
  beats they've witnessed but marked `hidden`, plus events that (by your chronology) have
  *already happened* but they haven't reached in reader-order.

The line beneath interprets the gap — flagging **dramatic irony** (when the reader thinks
they understand but you're withholding a lot) versus moments of **clarity**.

> This updates live as you add beats, flip reveal markers, or drag the timelines. It's the
> best way to check the reader is confused *exactly where you want* — and oriented where
> they need to be.

> 💡 Prefer the old simple view? Click **☰ Simple list** at the top of the Map to switch
> to a single draggable list (reader-order only). **⇋ Two-Track** switches back.

Click **"Lock the Blueprint →"** when done. (You can always come back and rearrange.)

## 8. Chamber 3 — The Desk

*(Phase III: Crafting the Illusion)*

This is where you **write your actual prose** — and where Vyasa's safety net runs.

### Your manuscript, scene by scene

The Desk is split in two:

- **Left — the Scene list.** Click **+ add** to create a scene. Each scene is its own
  passage (a chapter, a beat, a moment). Click any scene to open it; **drag** scenes to
  reorder them. A coloured dot means that scene has open contradictions.
- **Right — the Scene editor.** Give the scene a **title**, optionally **link it to a
  beat** from your Map (so your outline becomes your draft), and write your prose in the
  large area. A live **word count** and **scan state** sit beneath it.

*(If you used an earlier version with one big text box, don't worry — your writing was
automatically moved into "Scene 1." Nothing is lost.)*

### Scanning

Below the editor are the buttons:

- **🧩 Scan this Scene** → the main check on the current scene (works offline, instant).
- **✦ Deep Scan (Muse)** → a deeper AI check of the current scene (needs a key — see [section 11](#11-the-muse)).
- **⤢ Scan All Scenes** → runs the offline check across your whole manuscript at once.

When you scan, Vyasa compares the prose against everything in your Story Bible and fills
the **Contradiction Log** below — and **every finding names the scene it came from.**
Click the 🎬 scene chip on any finding to jump straight to that scene. This is the heart
of the tool, so it gets its own section 👇

## 9. The safety net

Three features work together to keep you from breaking your own story. Set up the
first two in the **Story Bible** (right panel); the third appears on the **Desk**.

### A) The Canon Ledger — your story's memory

In the Story Bible, find **Canon Ledger** and click **"+ entity"**.

An **entity** is anything you want to keep consistent: a **character**, **place**,
**object**, **fact**, or **timeline**. For each entity:
- **Rename it** (click the name, type).
- **Set its type** (click the little type label to cycle character → place → object → …).
- **Add facts** — type a fact and press Enter. E.g. for a character "Elias":
  - `left-handed`
  - `doesn't remember the year 1998`
  - `dead in timeline B`

These facts are now **tracked**. If your writing ever contradicts one, Vyasa flags it.

### B) Rules of the Anomaly — the laws of your world

Still in the Story Bible, under **Rules of the Anomaly**, type a rule and press Enter.
E.g. *"The time-loop only resets when the protagonist dies."*

Each rule then shows two small boxes — fill them in to make the rule **enforceable**:
- **Trigger** — the words/concept that mean the rule is in play (e.g. `loop reset`).
- **Limit** — what the rule forbids or requires (e.g. `only at death`).

A rule with both boxes filled is a rule Vyasa can actually *check*. (Rules also boost
your Integrity score more when they're fully specified.)

### C) The Contradiction Log — the alarm

On the **Desk**, after you click **Scan Passage**, this log fills with any problems
found. Each one is a card showing:
- **The problem** in plain English, e.g. *"Canon says Elias is 'left-handed,' but this
  passage writes 'right hand.'"*
- **A severity tag:**
  - 🔴 **hard break** — a clear factual contradiction. Almost always a mistake to fix.
  - 🟣 **check** — a possible issue worth a look (e.g. two opposite ideas sitting close together).
- **A quote** showing where in your text it found the issue.
- **Two buttons:**
  - **By Design** — *"This contradiction is intentional — it's my reveal."* The flag
    turns green and stops counting against you. **This is crucial:** mind-bending stories
    are *full* of deliberate contradictions. Vyasa doesn't assume you're wrong — it asks.
  - **Dismiss** — remove the flag entirely.

> 🧠 **The philosophy:** Vyasa never "fixes" your story or judges it. It **surfaces**
> every contradiction and lets *you* decide: mistake, or masterstroke? That's the
> difference between a story that's confusing-by-accident and one that's
> confusing-by-design.

### The Canon Integrity meter reacts

Every **open** (undecided) contradiction pulls your Integrity score **down** — a hard
break costs more than a soft check. Mark something **By Design** or fix it, and the
score climbs back. Watching that number is how you *feel* your labyrinth getting tighter.

> **Try this to see it work:**
> 1. Add entity "Elias" with the fact `left-handed`.
> 2. On the Desk, write: *"Elias signed the letter with his right hand."*
> 3. Click **Scan Passage**. → A red **hard break** appears; Integrity drops.
> 4. Click **By Design** (pretend it's a clue). → It goes green; Integrity recovers.

## 10. Chamber 4 — Launch

*(Phase IV: Launching the Labyrinth)*

Once your story exists, this chamber helps you **package** it without spoiling it:

- **The Hook of Confusion (blurb)** — your back-cover description. Aim for 150–200 words
  that tease the mystery and end on a question the reader *needs* answered. No spoilers.
- **Cover Brief** — describe a cover concept: mood, colours, a symbol. Keep it abstract;
  don't literally draw the twist.
- **Comp Titles & Keywords** — books/films like yours (your "comps") and search keywords.
  These help readers who love this genre find you.

The Muse can draft any of these for you from your Story Bible (next section).

### 📖 Bind the Manuscript (export)

At the bottom of Launch, Vyasa can **compile your scenes — in reader-order — into a
finished document**. It shows your scene count, word count, and reading time, then offers
four exports:

- **📄 Read / Print** — opens a beautifully formatted manuscript in a new tab (title page,
  chapter breaks, proper typography). Use your browser's **Print → Save as PDF** to keep it.
- **⌨ Markdown** — a `.md` file, ideal for pasting into Scrivener, Google Docs, or a Kindle
  publishing pipeline.
- **📝 Plain text** — a universal `.txt` of the whole manuscript.
- **🗂 Story-Bible Report** — one document containing *everything*: paradox, theme, rules,
  canon, beats, blurb, **and** the full manuscript. Perfect for sharing with a co-writer,
  editor, or your future self.

> The manuscript is assembled from your **scenes in reader-order** (the right-hand column
> of the Map / the order in the Desk sidebar) — so the reader experiences it exactly as you
> arranged. Scenes with no prose are skipped.

## 11. The Muse

*(Optional AI co-writer — bottom of the Story Bible panel)*

The Muse is an **optional** assistant that can suggest ideas or draft text. It's off by
default and **you don't need it** — the whole tool works without it.

### How to turn it on
1. You need a free **API key** from either **Google Gemini** or **Groq** (both offer
   free tiers). Get one from their websites.
2. In the Muse panel: choose your provider, **paste your key**.
3. Pick a task from the dropdown:
   - Suggest a sharper paradox
   - Propose non-linear beats
   - Write a dialogue of doubt
   - Draft the blurb
   - Cover concepts
4. Click **"Consult the Muse."**

It reads your **whole Story Bible** as context, so its suggestions fit *your* story.

The Muse also powers **Deep Scan** on the Desk — a smarter contradiction check that
catches subtle issues the instant offline scan can't.

### About your key (important & reassuring)
- Your key is stored **only in your browser**, and is sent **only** to the provider you
  chose (Google or Groq) — never to Vyasa or anyone else. Vyasa has no server.
- If you're on a shared computer, clear it when done (or use **Reset**).

## 12. Saving, the Vault & starting over

- **Saving:** Automatic. Every keystroke is saved to your browser's local storage.
- **The Vault (🛡 in the left rail):** your safety net and time machine — see below.
- **Exporting (⬇ in the left rail):** Downloads your entire Story Bible as a `.json`
  file. **Do this to back up your work or move it to another computer.**
- **Changing world (🎚️):** Returns you to the Threshold to pick a different genre skin.
  Your work is kept.
- **Reset (⟲):** ⚠️ **Wipes the current Bible** and starts fresh (asks first). Your Vault
  snapshots are kept, so you can restore afterwards.

### 🛡 The Vault — snapshots, backups & time travel

Open it from the **🛡** button in the left rail. Inside:

- **📸 Snapshot Now** — saves a named copy of your entire Story Bible. Take one before
  any big change (a rewrite, a risky restructure) so you can always come back.
- **Restore** — roll any snapshot back into the app. Restoring **auto-snapshots your
  current work first**, so it's always reversible — you can never paint yourself into a
  corner.
- **Autosaves** — Vyasa quietly keeps your recent states (a rolling set) as a safety net,
  even if you never take a manual snapshot. They appear in their own group.
- **⬆ Import .json** — load a previously exported Bible back in (your current work is
  auto-snapshotted first).
- **Delete** — remove a snapshot you no longer need.

> ⚠️ **Still export for real backups.** The Vault lives *in this browser*. Clearing your
> browser data / cache, or using private/incognito mode, can erase both your Bible **and**
> the Vault. For anything you'd hate to lose, **Export a `.json`** and keep it somewhere
> safe — that file is your true, portable save.

## 13. A full worked example

Let's build a tiny thriller from scratch so you see the whole flow.

**Step 1 — Threshold.** Open the app, click **Thriller**. The app turns taut and forensic.

**Step 2 — Genesis.**
- *Working Title:* `The Third Witness`
- *The Anomaly:* `A detective interrogates the only witness to a murder — and slowly
  realises the witness is himself, one week in the future, describing a crime the
  detective hasn't committed yet.`
- *Theme:* `Are we responsible for who we are about to become?`
- *Unreliability:* click **Fractured timeline** + **Memory-gapped narrator** (2 max).
- Click **Anchor the Paradox →**.

**Step 3 — Set up the safety net (Story Bible, right panel).**
- **Canon Ledger → + entity.** Name it `Detective Voss`, type `character`. Add facts:
  - `never carries a gun`
  - `left-handed`
- **Rules of the Anomaly → type:** `Future-Voss can only describe events, never change them.`
  Then fill **Trigger:** `future voss` and **Limit:** `never change`.

**Step 4 — The Map.** Add beats, press Enter after each:
- `Voss meets the witness` → set reveal to `hinted`
- `The witness knows the victim's name before it's released` → `hidden`
- `Voss sees the witness is wearing his own watch` → `revealed`
Drag them so the *watch* beat comes last in reading order, even if it "happens" early.
Click **Lock the Blueprint →**.

**Step 5 — The Desk.** Write:
> *"Voss slid the file across with his right hand. 'Tell me again,' he said. The witness
> smiled and reached to change the record — and the room reset."*

Click **🧩 Scan Passage.** Vyasa flags:
- 🔴 **hard break:** *"Canon says Detective Voss is 'left-handed,' but this passage writes 'right hand.'"*
- 🟣 **check:** the rule `Future-Voss can only describe events, never change them` — its
  trigger appears but the passage shows him *changing* the record.

**Step 6 — Decide.**
- The left/right hand is a genuine slip → edit the text to `left hand`, re-scan, it clears.
- The "change the record" flag → maybe that's your big reveal that the rules are breaking!
  Click **By Design.** It goes green.

Watch the **Canon Integrity** meter climb as you resolve each one.

**Step 7 — Launch.** Go to the Launch chamber, write a teasing blurb (or click the Muse
to draft one), add comps like `Memento, Dark, Tenet`.

**Step 8 — Export.** Click ⬇ to download `The_Third_Witness.json`. You're done — and your
labyrinth is airtight.

## 14. FAQ & troubleshooting

**Q: I came back and the app jumps me somewhere / looks themed already.**
The homepage always loads first now, but it *remembers your last world* and offers a
**"Continue"** button. Click it to resume, or pick a fresh genre.

**Q: I made changes but don't see them / the page looks old.**
Your browser cached an old version. Do a **hard refresh**: `Ctrl+Shift+R` (Windows/Linux)
or `Cmd+Shift+R` (Mac). Or open it in a private window.

**Q: The Scan found nothing, but I know there's a contradiction.**
The instant (offline) scan catches factual clashes it has been taught (opposite states,
canon facts, rule triggers). For subtler, meaning-level contradictions, use **✦ Deep
Scan** with a Muse key. Also make sure you've actually **added the relevant fact** to the
Canon Ledger — Vyasa can only guard what it knows.

**Q: The Scan flagged something that's totally fine.**
That's expected sometimes — it errs on the side of showing you things. Just click
**By Design** or **Dismiss**. Your decision sticks across future scans.

**Q: Is my work private?**
Yes. Nothing leaves your browser except (a) Muse requests you trigger, which go only to
your chosen AI provider. There is no Vyasa server storing anything.

**Q: Will I lose my work?**
Only if you clear browser data, use incognito, or click Reset. **Export regularly** to be safe.

**Q: Does it work on my phone?**
It's designed for a desktop/laptop screen (three columns + drag-and-drop). It'll open on
mobile but the experience is much better on a larger screen.

**Q: Do I have to use the Muse / an API key?**
No. Every core feature — chambers, canon, rules, offline contradiction scan, integrity
meter, export — works with **no key at all**.
