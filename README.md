## What it is

You don't "start a project." You cross a **threshold** by naming the world you'll bend —
**Sci-Fi · Horror · Thriller · Fantasy** — and the entire instrument re-skins live:
palette, typography, motion, film-grain, even the voice Vyasa speaks in.

Then you move through **four chambers** — the four phases of building a labyrinth —
while a single, persistent **Story Bible** remembers everything so the maze stays coherent.

| Chamber | Phase | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 🌀 **Genesis** | The Genesis of Disorientation | Forge the **Central Paradox**, theme, and 1–2 unreliability mechanisms (capped — restraint is enforced). |
| 🗺️ **The Map** | The Labyrinth's Blueprint | Add beats, then **drag** them into *reader-order*. Each carries a `hidden → hinted → revealed` marker. |
| 🎭 **The Desk** | Crafting the Illusion | Write — while the **Coherence engine** audits your prose against your own declared rules. |
| 📢 **Launch** | Launching the Labyrinth | Draft the blurb, cover brief, comps & keywords. |

### The two ideas that make it different

1. **Genre-reactive theming.** The tool doesn't just *hold* your genre — it *becomes* it.
2. **Coherence over chaos.** A live meter + a rules-of-the-anomaly ledger keep your absurdity
   *designed*, never accidental. Vyasa keeps the rules so your reader gets lost — and you never do.

## Run it

It's a single self-contained HTML file. No build, no install, no backend.

```bash
# just open it
open index.html            # macOS
xdg-open index.html        # Linux

# or serve locally
python3 -m http.server 3000
```

Your **Story Bible** persists in the browser (localStorage) and exports to JSON any time.

## The Muse (optional AI co-writer)

Bring your **own** API key (Gemini or Groq). It stays in *your* browser and is sent only to
the provider you choose — never to any Vyasa server (there isn't one). The Muse reads your
whole Story Bible as context to suggest paradoxes, non-linear beats, dialogues of doubt,
blurbs, and cover concepts.

## For screenwriters

Building your next film the way Nolan builds a puzzle? Vyasa treats a screenplay as a labyrinth
too — anchor the paradox, drag your reveals out of chronological order, and let the Desk guard
your time-loop rules while you write.
